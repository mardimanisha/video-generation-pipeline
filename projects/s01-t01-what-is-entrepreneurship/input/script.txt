Welcome to the first lesson of our entrepreneurship course. Today we begin with a simple but powerful question. What is entrepreneurship?

You have probably heard this word many times. It often brings to mind famous founders, big startups, and huge success stories. But entrepreneurship is much broader than that. And it is much closer to everyday life than most people think.

Let's start with a clear definition. Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying a problem or an unmet need, and creating a solution that delivers value to people. An entrepreneur is someone who takes the initiative to make that happen.

Notice what this definition does not say. It does not say you need a large amount of money. It does not say you need a big company. And it does not say you need a completely new invention. At its heart, entrepreneurship is about noticing problems and doing something useful about them.

To understand this better, we will use a simple framework with four steps. Problem, opportunity, solution, and value.

The first step is the problem. Every entrepreneurial journey starts with something that is not working well. It might be a task that takes too long, or a product that costs too much. It might be a service that is difficult to access. Problems are everywhere, if you learn to look for them.

The second step is the opportunity. Not every problem is an opportunity. A problem becomes an opportunity when many people experience it, when it matters to them, and when they would welcome a better way. In other words, an opportunity is a problem worth solving.

The third step is the solution. This is the product, service, or process that addresses the problem. A good solution is not just clever. It is practical and affordable, and it genuinely makes people's lives easier.

The fourth step is value. Value is the benefit people receive from the solution. It might save them time, save them money, or reduce their stress. When a solution creates real value, people will pay for it, use it, and recommend it.

Let's look at an example. Think about how difficult it once was to find a taxi late at night. You had to stand on the street and hope that one would pass by. That was the problem. Millions of people faced it every day, so it was a real opportunity. Ride hailing apps offered a solution by connecting riders and drivers through a smartphone. The value was convenience, safety, and predictability.

Here is a smaller example. A student notices that classmates struggle to find healthy, affordable lunches near campus. She starts preparing simple meal boxes and takes orders through a messaging group. The problem is clear, and the need is real. The solution is simple, and the value is obvious. That is entrepreneurship too.

So why does entrepreneurship matter? There are four important reasons.

First, entrepreneurship drives innovation. New ideas often begin when someone asks, is there a better way?

Second, entrepreneurship creates jobs and growth. Growing ventures hire people and support local suppliers.

Third, it helps solve social problems. Many entrepreneurs work on clean water, education, and healthcare.

Finally, it builds valuable personal skills. Even if you never start a company, thinking like an entrepreneur helps in any career.

Let's bring everything together. Entrepreneurship is not only about starting a business. It is a way of thinking. It begins with noticing a problem and recognising an opportunity. It continues with designing a solution that creates value for others.

Remember this simple chain. Problem, opportunity, solution, value. We will return to it again and again throughout this course.

Now it is your turn. Over the next day, pay close attention to your daily routine, and identify one everyday problem you notice. It could be at home, at work, in your community, or on your way to class. Write it down, and ask yourself who else experiences this problem. That simple habit is the first step toward thinking like an entrepreneur.
